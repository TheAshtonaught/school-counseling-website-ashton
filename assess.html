Assess

Assess asks a simple but essential question: Is our school counseling program making a meaningful difference for students?

What It Means

What Does Assess Mean?

The Assess component uses data and reflection to evaluate the effectiveness of the Houston High School Comprehensive School Counseling Program.

Assessment helps the school counselor identify student needs, evaluate interventions, measure progress toward program goals, recognize areas of success, and identify opportunities for improvement. It helps ensure that decisions are based not only on what we believe students need, but also on evidence of what students are experiencing and accomplishing.

Professional Reflection

School Counselor Self-Assessment

The ASCA School Counselor Professional Standards & Competencies Assessment provides a structured way to reflect on professional mindsets and behaviors and to identify areas for continued growth.

Mindsets I Bring to the Work

I believe students are capable of growth and that every student deserves access to meaningful opportunities, support, and a clear pathway toward life after high school. I approach school counseling from a strengths-based perspective and believe students benefit when adults help them recognize possibilities they may not yet see for themselves.

Professional Behaviors I Continue to Develop

My six years as an educator have strengthened my communication, collaboration, student advocacy, relationship-building, and understanding of academic expectations. As I transition into school counseling, I will continue strengthening my use of school counseling data, program leadership, community partnerships, and systematic program evaluation.

Continuous improvement: Self-assessment helps identify professional growth goals and guides ongoing learning throughout the school year.
Program Goals

From Student Need to Measurable Action

School counseling program goals should begin with identifiable student needs rather than with activities alone.

1. Identify a Need

Review student, school, and stakeholder data to identify an area where students may need additional support.

2. Develop a Measurable Goal

Create a specific goal connected to a student outcome, such as attendance, achievement, graduation progress, belonging, or postsecondary readiness.

3. Deliver Targeted Support

Select school counseling activities or interventions that address the identified need.

4. Review Results

Analyze data at the end of the intervention or school year to determine what changed, who benefited, and what should be adjusted.

Survey Data

Listening to Students and Stakeholders

Surveys provide perception data about what students, families, teachers, and other stakeholders believe students need and what they learned from school counseling services.

Needs Assessment

Survey data may identify concerns involving college and career readiness, stress, school belonging, academic confidence, postsecondary knowledge, access to resources, or social/emotional needs.

Program Planning

Results help the school counselor prioritize services and decide where targeted instruction, advisement, counseling, collaboration, or other supports may be needed.

End-of-Year Assessment

Follow-up surveys can help determine whether students gained knowledge, skills, confidence, or awareness and can inform improvements for the next school year.

Data We Use

How We Measure Program Impact

Participation Data

Who participated in a school counseling activity or intervention? Participation data helps show the reach of the program and whether students are accessing services equitably.

Mindsets & Behaviors Data

What knowledge, attitudes, or skills did students learn through participation in school counseling activities?

Outcome Data

Did attendance, academic achievement, discipline, graduation progress, or other student outcomes change after the intervention?

Additional data may include grades, credits earned, course failures, graduation rates, discipline referrals, postsecondary applications, financial aid completion, advanced course enrollment, career certification completion, and student participation.

Attendance & Achievement

Why These Data Matter

Attendance Data

Attendance affects a student's opportunity to learn. Analyzing attendance data helps identify students or groups experiencing barriers to regular attendance and reveals patterns that may require additional support. The school counselor can then collaborate with students, families, educators, administrators, and community partners to address those barriers and evaluate whether interventions improve attendance.

Achievement Data

Achievement data helps identify which students are succeeding academically and which students may need additional support. Grades, credits, course completion, graduation progress, and other academic indicators can reveal patterns and disparities. These data help the school counselor target interventions, advocate for students, monitor progress, and evaluate whether school counseling services are contributing to improved outcomes.
