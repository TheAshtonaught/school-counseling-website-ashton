Deliver

Deliver explains how students receive direct school counseling services and how school counselors support students indirectly through families, educators, and community partners.

What It Means

What Does Deliver Mean?

The Deliver component represents the services students receive through the Houston High School Comprehensive School Counseling Program.

Services are delivered through both direct student services and indirect student services. Together, these services support academic development, career development, and social/emotional development while helping students prepare for success beyond high school.

Direct Student Services

Services With Students

I

Instruction

Structured school counseling lessons help students develop knowledge and skills. Topics may include college and career readiness, graduation planning, goal-setting, study skills, financial aid, postsecondary options, decision-making, healthy relationships, and stress management.

A

Appraisal

Appraisal helps students better understand themselves by examining interests, strengths, academic performance, skills, values, career interests, and postsecondary options. The school counselor helps students interpret this information and connect it to future decisions.

A

Advisement

Advisement supports informed academic and career decisions. It may include course selection, graduation monitoring, postsecondary planning, college exploration, technical education, military pathways, admissions requirements, and next-step planning.

C

Counseling

School counseling provides short-term individual or small-group support for academic, career, and social/emotional concerns such as stress, relationships, grief, transitions, confidence, motivation, and decision-making. When needs exceed the scope of school counseling, the school counselor helps connect students and families with appropriate outside support.

Indirect Student Services

Services On Behalf of Students

Consultation

The school counselor consults with parents, teachers, administrators, and other professionals to better understand student needs and identify strategies that may support student success.

Collaboration

The school counselor collaborates with families, educators, community organizations, colleges, employers, and other stakeholders to expand opportunities and coordinate support for students.

Referrals

When students need services beyond the scope of the school counseling program, the school counselor helps students and families identify appropriate community resources, mental health services, social services, medical providers, or other specialized supports.

Accessing Services

How to Get Support

For Students: How to Access School Counseling Services

Students are encouraged to reach out whenever they need academic, career, or social/emotional support.

Visit the Houston High School School Counseling Office before school, during lunch, or after school.
Ask a teacher or another trusted staff member to help connect you with the school counselor.
Complete a Student School Counseling Request Form available through the School Counseling Office.
Request an appointment directly with the school counselor.
Immediate safety concern? Tell a trusted adult right away rather than waiting for a scheduled appointment.

For Parents & Guardians: How to Access the School Counselor

Parents and guardians are important partners in student success and may request support on behalf of their child.

Contact the Houston High School School Counseling Office and request to speak with the school counselor.
Request a phone, virtual, or in-person conference regarding your child.
Ask a school staff member to connect you with the appropriate school counselor.
Request support related to academics, graduation, college and career readiness, social/emotional concerns, or community resources.
